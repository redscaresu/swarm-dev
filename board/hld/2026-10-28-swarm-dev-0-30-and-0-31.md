# swarm-dev 0.30 and 0.31

One design, two releases: v0.30.0 makes the swarm's loop end its reviews, reach its agents and
wake the lead; v0.31.0 fixes what the v0.29.0 build broke around it and closes the 0.28 HLD's last
stage. It takes over the content of the 0.28 HLD's v0.30.0 and v0.31.0 rows (Rollout § Stages)
and keeps that HLD's five draft epics where they are. The 0.28 HLD stays `agreed`: its epics'
stage opening and ticket depend on it (bin/lib/board.sh:1075-1076, 1312-1328), so it is never
marked `superseded` while one of them is open.

## Problem

## Non-goals

- **Changing how the top rung runs**, a usage gate on the ladder, running the swarm from main, a
  guard on hand-written polling loops: the 0.28 HLD's non-goals stand. Its designs stand too,
  except where Design here names the change (Design § Review rounds end moves one line of
  0.28 § Test speed).
- **Replanning the five adopted epics.** Their files, Done when and stages stand (Design §
  The 0.28 epics).
- **A new review role.** The review agent decides decline-only replies and the cap; no separate
  decider, no second reviewer.
- **Conductor lanes**, or more than one conductor per epic.
- **A herdr change, or a change to hook timing.** herdr's state for a pane with a background
  shell is read around in `tell_state` and `agent_stalled`, and herdr itself is left alone; the agent-state
  hook's timeouts were raised in v0.29.0 (#622), and #694's fix reads the recorded session id
  when herdr has none rather than making herdr hold it.
- **Changing `tell` before the queue has run.** Problem 2 shows the queue untried; v0.30.0
  logs why each tell is refused and reads the first build before touching delivery.
- **The corporate machine.** Defender scanning and the user's own containers are outside.
- **Real cloud, infrafactory or any project other than swarm-dev.** As in 0.28: a change still
  reaches every board on upgrade, so each release PR lists the behaviour changes every project
  gets.

## Design

Each part records the user's decision and its date. On 2026-10-28 the user accepted the
reviewer's findings in advance; where a finding offered a choice, the option taken is named
with the reason, and the user can move it. The plan is drawn from this section; epics and
stories name the mechanism.

### Stage contents (decided 2026-10-28)

The lead's split (`.swarm/lead/v030-inputs.md`), with #693 and #696 moved into v0.30.0 because
they are small and safety-related. v0.30.0 holds what changes the loop the next build runs on;
v0.31.0 holds what that build then benefits from.

Why two releases and not patches: the patch cadence (Current state) carries a fix only while
every change since the tag is a fix. v0.30.0's delivery-and-liveness fixes can ride as v0.29.x
patches until the first feature merges; the review loop, the telemetry fields and the rules
file are features and wait for the minor. So the plan orders the fix epic first (the feature
epics depend on it), the lead cuts a patch once it has merged, and the cadence then pauses
until v0.30.0. While #694 stands, a patch moves newly started agents only. docs/reference.md's
split clashes with almost every story and goes after the loop stories have merged.

**The stage gate needs a check.** Nothing refuses an active epic whose `stage:` is above the
open one (Current state § Staging), so one misread by the planner writes the reference.md
split active mid-v0.30.0. A v0.30.0 story adds the check: `board-pr` and `merge --board` refuse
a PR that sets an epic `active` whose `stage:` is above the lowest stage `next` has opened. For
this HLD's own plan PR, which lands before that story, the lead reads each epic's `status:`
against its `stage:` before `merge --board`, and says so in the merge.

Why: /plan-hld cannot change an existing epic, the stage machinery reads `stage:` from any

### Review rounds end (decided 2026-10-28)

- **A round cap from the PR's third findings round, counted across rungs.** The count is new:
  findings tells delivered for the story since its first build start, step-ups included
  (today's `review_rounds` resets on a step-up and `findings_told` is per rung, Current state).
  On this board, with `step_up_findings` 3, the PR's third round is also the first rung's
  step-up round, so from the second rung on every round sends only narrowed findings. From
  that round the review agent sends only findings that are a regression this PR introduces or
  a line now false about the code, and declines the rest as deferred, logged with
  `log-finding --declined deferred`, so #700's successor list writes itself from
  `findings.tsv`. `deferred` is a new reason: today `log-finding` accepts only `out-of-scope`,
  `nit` and `wrong` (bin/lib/reports.sh), so the cap's story adds it to `log-finding` and to
  the readers of `findings.tsv` before the cap sends anything. The cap narrows what a round may send; it never merges a PR with a regression
  in it. The data behind 3: rounds three and later (per rung) fixed 119 of 312 findings in
  v0.29.0. Unmeasured: how many of those 119 were regressions or false lines, which the cap
  keeps, and how many it would have deferred. That is the first reading (Rollout § Readings):
  deferred findings per PR, and how many of them a later round or the retro showed real.
- **Declines carry forward, as data.** Each codex round is given the earlier rounds' findings
  and the builder's reason for each, marked as the builder's claim, the way the briefs hand
  over CI output (merge.sh:1564, 1570). Codex is not told to ignore anything. The review agent,
  not codex, drops a repeat of a decline it accepted, as the brief already has it re-decline.
  So a decline written to cover a class of findings suppresses nothing by itself.
- **The review agent decides a decline-only reply**, in its session, as the brief already says:
  read the reply, accept the pass when every reason holds, or send one more round. No new role:
  it has the thread, it is one per PR so nothing queues, and the rule shipped in #650. What
  v0.30.0 adds is the proof: a test that the path is taken, and a `decline-only` event with the
  round number, so the retro can count it and spot a builder that declines everything to end
  the loop.
- **`review_verify = ci | full`, default `ci`.** The brief never asks the review agent to run a
  test (Current state), so the 20-to-50-minute rounds were the agent's habit, and 0.28 § Test
  speed already decided that a review round runs the suite once, in CI. The key the user asked
  for (#686) stays, with values that say something: `ci` means every round reads the diff and
  trusts CI, no local suite and no mutation run, written as one line in the review brief;
  `full` means round one may run the selected tests for the changed files and check the
  builder's mutation proofs, and rounds two and later are `ci`. The default is `ci`: 0.28 already
  decided it, and a `full` default would turn an unasked habit into shipped
  behaviour. The user first chose `full` as default with `ci` on this board (2026-10-28,
  before the review); the reviewer's finding moved it, and the user accepted. No board sets
  the key today, so no board changes on upgrade. The alternative weighed: one brief line and no
  key. Rejected because the user asked for the setting and `full` has a use on a board whose
  CI is slow or thin.
- **The ladder stands.** The cap is a rule on what a round sends, not a change to
  `step_up_findings` or `top_rung_rounds`.

### Delivery and liveness (decided 2026-10-28)

- **Why a tell was refused is logged**, and `tell` itself does not change in v0.30.0. The queue
  shipped in #626 and has never run in a build; the agent-side refusals (229 of 410) were on
  old code. Telemetry item 1 records the state `tell_state` saw, and the v0.31.0 checkpoint
  reads it. If the first build still loses tells to a pane with a background shell, that is a
  v0.31.0 story with the data behind it.
- **A dead agent is told once.** A tell or nudge to an agent that is gone is refused with a
  `wait --lead` line naming it and its exit (`conduct <epic>` again, or `resume <slug>`, or
  nothing when its story left the board). No swarm.sh command retries it, and the lead's brief
  says the lead does not either.
- **Stall detection reads the builder's state.** `agent_stalled` calls `bg_running`, one call to code
  that exists: a live background task is work. A sign failure and a flagged pane are
  not a stall either (Open question 3 for the GPG signal).
- **A failed start retries.** `review-agent` and `story` retry a start that failed with a
  closed pane or `agent_not_found` up to twice, then the `no-review:` line names the restart
  command, which the lead can run or `wait --lead` offers (#688).
- **Five stopgap behaviours become `wait --lead` lines**, each with its exit: a stuck builder
  (idle, PR head unchanged N minutes, findings or conflict pending: `resume <slug>` with the
  pending item); `main-red:` for a red base whoever merged, with the `fix-main` story as the
  exit; a missing review agent, restarted by the line's own retry; a conductor that reported
  with stories left (`conduct <epic>` again); and `merge` and `wait --lead` re-reading the
  board after a concurrent merge deleted a story file. Signals must be true: each line is tested
  against the false case as well as the real one, as 0.28 required. The API-error case is left
  out because its line exists (Current state).
- **`next` sends a built, unfinished epic to a conductor.** An epic whose stories all merged
  with no report still needs that report, so `next` prints `conduct <epic>` for it instead of
  `plan-epic`. The conductor that starts writes the report and the Finish PR, as a conductor
  with no agent work left already does, so no new role ends an epic.
- **Not copied from the stopgaps:** direct pane prompts, pass detection by regex, skipping
  findings the ladder handled, a retry loop on a gone agent.

### Telemetry (decided 2026-10-28)

- **The format rule.** New fields go inside the fifth column, after the outcome, as
  `key=value` pairs separated by spaces, so every reader that keeps rows with five fields
  (Current state § Events) still reads every row, old rows read as having no field, and other
  projects' files under `dash --project` need nothing. A reader that wants a field parses the
  column, and no sixth column is added. Five columns are not enough on their own: readers such as
  flow_holds.py, outcomes_rows.py and dash_age.py compare the fifth field exactly with `ok` or
  match `ok (round N)`. So the telemetry epic first moves every reader of the outcome onto one
  helper that strips the `key=value` tail, with a test that an old row and a row with fields
  read the same, and only then lets a writer add a field.
- **Nine fields**, in the lead's order with one dropped: why a tell was refused (the state
  `tell_state` saw); slug and role on incident, reap, guard-blocked and merge-blocked events;
  round number and verdict on review events, and a `review-finished` event; PR-opened and
  first-green events; a commit or sign failure event (Open question 3 names its writer); a
  cause on lead-hand and user-caught; the plugin version on every event; the builder's
  activity on a wait-timeout, logged only when there is no progress; and a `decline-only`
  reply event. Dropped: a builder's test-run start and end per story, because
  `.swarm/test-runs.tsv` already records each run and `flow`'s `test_runs` reads it; the plan
  adds the story slug to that file's row if it is missing, not a second record.
- `flow` reads each field, so the next retro's numbers come from one command.

### Safety in v0.30.0 (decided 2026-10-28)

- **A merge of main may not drop main's lines** (#693). The check compares the merge commit's
  own resolution against the PR before it: a base line the merge commit removes (as
  `git show --remerge-diff` lists it, the check the lead ran) must be one the PR's non-merge
  commits already removed. The diff `base...head` after the merge cannot serve: it lists every
  missing base line as the PR's own. The review agent runs it before it reads the diff and stops
  the round with a line to the builder naming the dropped line; `swarm.sh merge` runs it before
  it merges and holds the PR with the same line. A line the PR means to remove passes because
  its own commits removed it.
- **Two rules in the rules file** (#696): never generate load on the swarm machine, never read
  `~/.claude/projects` or any session log. Checks over prose where one exists: the load rule
  already has `load_rule`; the session-log rule is prose until a check is cheap.

### v0.31.0: the machine, merge mechanics and the retro (decided 2026-10-28)

Each item names its mechanism, so the epic that takes it traces here.

- **docs/reference.md splits so that two stories touch two files.** The plan first reads where
  the 92 merge-ins fell (the merge commits that touched reference.md, by section) and chooses
  the split by that evidence: by command group if they fell across groups, by section within
  the hot group if inside one. The cost is named: eight anchors into `reference.md#...` and
  tests/doc_check_test.sh move with it, in the same PR.
- **The test cap weighs runs.** scripts/test.sh counts a selected run as a fraction of a slot
  (the plan picks the weight from `test-runs.tsv`'s durations); the cap stays off by default.
- **Finished panes close** (#690): a review pane after its merge or handover note, by reconcile
  and tidy, which already close panes; a planning agent's pane once its output is read; a
  conductor's pane after its Finish PR has merged, never before (closing it sooner kills that
  merge, docs/lead-by-hand.md:51-56).
- **Builders push once** (#695): after the PR opens, mutation proofs run on a copy of the
  worktree without `.git`, never in place; before it opens, in place with cp stands. This
  changes `mutation_rule` (build.sh:308-310) and AGENTS.md:184-187, and both move in the same
  PR. The brief adds: push what is ready, put further points in the PR body. A review that
  stopped on a moving head is rerun by its conductor's `stopped:` handling, not left.
- **Merge mechanics**: #691 (`merge` and `pr_stories` re-read the board after a pull when a
  story file is missing); #692 (`swarm.sh merge` takes a lead PR outside a story when the
  lead's `review` listed it under a lead story, and `next` names one that is not); #697
  (`merge --board` of a plan PR runs `conduct` for each epic the merge set active); #694
  (`upgrade` reads the recorded session id from agents.tsv when herdr has none, skips agents
  whose pane is closed, and `merge --release` asks before running it); #699 is a `kind: lead`
  investigation story with a **You:** step, since its cause is unknown and no mechanism can be
  named.
- **Flaky tests** #698 (watch_opened_test's temp-dir cleanup), queue_test and codex_test's
  timing case are fixed, so a rerun is no longer the step.
- **#700's deferred findings** are listed one by one in the epic that takes them, each as a
  story or a drop with a reason, so the list is on the board before any story is filed.
- **The 0.28 HLD's last stage ships**: retro-gate, retro-report, handover-usage as planned,
  with the retro over v0.27.2..v0.31.0.
- **The `later` and orphaned stories**: codex-agent-name ships or is dropped; release-v0-29-0
  is closed by the lead now; ppid-check-after-review, sonnet-share-on and
  unsent-resend-real-pane are re-homed under a v0.31.0 epic or closed with a reason in the
  plan; the seven `kind: lead` steps are the user's and are closed or re-dated at the retro,
  as retro-report's story says.

## Alternatives

- **One release.** Rejected: the review loop, the telemetry and the rules file are features
  and cannot ship as patches, so the v0.31.0 build would run without them, and reference.md's
  split would clash with every story in flight.
- **Three or more releases.** Rejected: each cut costs a docs pass, a restart and a day; two
  matches the two kinds of work (the loop, then what runs on it).
- **Patches only, no v0.30.0 minor.** Rejected: the release arm cuts a patch only while every
  change since the tag is a fix, and the stage carries features.
- **Replan the five 0.28 epics under this HLD.** Rejected: /plan-hld cannot change them, their
  Done when lines still hold, and replanning spends planning agents for no new decision.
- **A separate decider for decline-only replies** (a second reviewer, or the conductor).
  Rejected: the review agent already owns the loop and the brief already says it decides; what
  is missing is the proof it does.
- **A hard round cap that merges at round N regardless.** Rejected: a regression found at
  round four is still a regression. The cap narrows what a round may send.
- **A per-rung round cap.** Rejected: on this board the third tell on a rung is the step-up,
  so a per-rung cap would never hold across rungs.
- **One brief line instead of `review_verify`.** Rejected: the user asked for the setting
  (#686), and `full` has a use on a board with slow or thin CI. The brief line is written
  either way; the key picks which.
- **Changing `tell` now for the background-shell case.** Rejected: no run shows the queue
  losing tells; v0.30.0 logs why and v0.31.0 reads it.
- **Fix herdr's "working" state.** Rejected for this design: herdr is a separate project;
  `tell_state` already overrides herdr on session evidence.
- **Keep the lead's stopgap scripts as the mechanism.** Rejected: they bypassed the round count,
  died unnoticed, nudged two deleted stories 455 times and had their own bugs (retro theme
  15). Each becomes a `wait --lead` line or is dropped.
- **A sixth events column.** Rejected: ten readers keep five-field rows, and other projects'
  files would need the same change; fields inside the outcome column cost nothing to old
  readers.
- **Checking a base merge with `git diff base...head`.** Rejected: it cannot fail (Design §
  Safety).

## Risks and safety

- **No user step between agreement and `main`.** With `epics` and `stories` granted and
  `merge = agent`, the chain from agreement to code on `main` has no user step but
  `kind: lead` stories, `costs: money` and the protected-path hold. Two exposures the reviewer
  named: a plan that writes a v0.31.0 epic `active` has no check until the stage-gate story
  lands (Design § Stage contents says what the lead checks by hand first); and no checker says
  an epic traces to a Design part (the plan critic checks the reverse), so Design § v0.31.0
  names a mechanism per item and the one investigation (#699) is a lead story. The user's way
  in is unchanged: `swarm.sh hold`, or `merge = human`.
- **`stories` files #700's items unasked.** The epic that takes them lists each with its
  story or drop, so the list is on the board before any story is; the user reads the epic.
- **A round cap can let a real finding through.** The cap keeps regressions and false lines;
  everything else is deferred and logged, so #700's successor lists it. The checkpoint reads
  deferred findings per PR and how many proved real.
- **A builder's declines reach codex.** They go as data, marked as the builder's claim, and
  codex is told to ignore nothing; the review agent decides what is a repeat. A decline the
  review agent accepted wrongly still suppresses that one point in later rounds, which is
  today's behaviour too.
- **`review_verify = ci` by default trusts CI from round one.** A flaky or slow CI then costs
  a round trip. 0.28 decided it; `full` is one line in a board's config, and `flow`'s
  `ci_health` shows the cost.
- **A dropped-lines check can refuse a correct merge** when the builder resolved a conflict by
  removing a base line on purpose in the merge commit itself. The line names the drop; the
  builder removes it in a commit of its own or answers in the PR.
- **Telemetry fields in the outcome column** can collide with outcome text that contains `=`.
  The plan picks a separator the existing outcomes do not use, with a test over today's file.
- **Patches during the v0.30.0 build move new agents only** while #694 stands.
- **The stale `release-v0-29-0` story** shows a cut can leave a `later` story behind unseen;
  the release stories' Done when should close themselves, which the plan adds to
  release-v0-30-0 and release-v0-31-0.
- **One stage ships before the retro gate.** v0.30.0 releases on the docs gate; the v0.31.0
  retro covers it, as 0.28 planned.

## Rollout

### Stages

Two minor releases, cut by the lead through `merge --release` with the release stories already
on the board (release-v0-30-0, release-v0-31-0). The 0.28 HLD's staging mechanism applies:
this HLD's epics carry `stage:`, v0.30.0's go `active` at planning, v0.31.0's `draft` until
`next` prints `stage v0.31.0` after the v0.30.0 tag. Each release holds exactly its stage.
Before that, the fix epic's merges ship as a v0.29.x patch (Design § Stage contents).

| Stage | Goals | What ships | Why this order |
|---|---|---|---|
| v0.30.0 | 1 to 9 | Review rounds end; delivery and liveness; telemetry; the stage-gate check; #693 and #696; rules-file and ladder-cost (0.28) | The v0.31.0 build runs on the fixed loop, with the telemetry to prove it |
| v0.31.0 | 10 to 16 | reference.md split; the machine (#690, #695, the cap); merge mechanics (#691, #692, #694, #697, #699); flaky tests; #700; retro-gate, retro-report, handover-usage (0.28); the `later` and orphaned stories | Built on the v0.30.0 loop; reference.md's split waits until the loop stories are in; the retro gate goes last so its retro covers everything since v0.27.2 |

**You:** restart the lead session after each release, as the release stories say.

### Readings

The v0.30.0 build runs on the v0.29.0 plugin, so it measures the old loop; its `flow` output
is a second baseline beside v0.29.0's, and release-v0-30-0's step 2 records it as that. The
first build on the new loop is v0.31.0's, about 40 stories. Two readings:

- **A checkpoint**, a `kind: lead` story in the v0.31.0 plan: after the first 20 story merges of
  v0.31.0, or seven days, whichever comes first, the lead runs `flow` once and records Goals 1
  to 9 against their bars. A goal off its bar files a story in the same stage; a feature that
  made things worse (more red CI under `review_verify = ci`, more deferred findings that proved
  real) is turned back by its config line and the retro decides.
- **The retro** (retro-report's story) reads everything over v0.27.2..v0.31.0, against the
  features.tsv bars.
